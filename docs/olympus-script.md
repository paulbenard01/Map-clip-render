# Olympus: The Mountain Nobody Climbed — script

A 2:30 script about Mount Olympus joining the World Heritage List
(Busan, 26 July 2026). This isn't a deep dive. It follows one thread: the
mountain was listed for a **belief**, not a building, and that raises the
question of what World Heritage is actually protecting.

Roughly 390 words of narration, about 155 wpm with room for pauses.

---

## Why this angle

Olympus's cultural case rests on a single criterion, **(vi)**: association
with "ideas, or with beliefs, with artistic and literary works". There is no
monument criterion. The Operational Guidelines say (vi) should "preferably be
used in conjunction with other criteria". Here its only partner is the
biodiversity criterion (x). So the cultural half of the listing is
essentially a myth.

That one fact links the three strongest pieces of the story:

1. **The myth and the rock were never the same place.** Homer's Olympus has
   no wind, rain or snow. The real one is snowbound for much of the year.
   Ancient worshippers climbed to a lower peak and stopped there. Nobody is
   recorded on the true summit until 1913, a year after the mountain came
   under Greek rule.
2. **UNESCO's own experts balked at exactly this.** The advisory bodies
   recommended *referral*. They wanted the cultural case clarified and Dion,
   a site with real archaeology, added. The Committee overrode them and
   inscribed Olympus unanimously.
3. **The ending writes itself.** The mountain was sacred because it was out
   of reach, and the listing's job is to bring people to it.

## Script

**[0:00–0:12] HOOK**

For nearly three thousand years, this was the most famous mountain on Earth.
The home of Zeus.

And for almost all of that time, nobody stood on the top.

**[0:12–0:32] THE NEWS**

This July, in Busan, UNESCO added Mount Olympus to the World Heritage List:
Greece's highest peak, 2,918 metres, after a twelve-year campaign.

But look at *why*. Its nature ticks one box: almost two thousand kinds of
plant, twenty-seven found nowhere else on Earth. Its culture ticks exactly
one other: criterion six. The one for ideas and beliefs.

**[0:32–0:55] THE STRANGE PART**

There's no temple here and no palace. Olympus is on the list because of what
people once *believed* about it.

And even the believers didn't quite mean this mountain. Homer's Olympus is
never shaken by wind, never wet with rain, and snow never touches it. The
real one spends much of the year buried in snow.

**[0:55–1:28] TWO MOUNTAINS**

The ancient Greeks did climb. On Agios Antonios, a peak just below the
summit, archaeologists found ash, coins, and stones inscribed to Olympian
Zeus. Priests came up, made their offerings, and went back down.

The highest point, Mytikas, stayed empty. The first recorded ascent was on
2 August 1913, by Christos Kakkalos, a local goat hunter, guiding two Swiss
photographers. That was one year after this mountain stopped being Ottoman
and became Greek.

So for most of history there were two Olympuses: the rock, and the idea.
They only really met in the twentieth century.

**[1:28–1:58] THE ARGUMENT**

That gap is exactly what UNESCO's own experts struggled with. Before the
vote, its advisory bodies recommended sending the file back to Greece. They
asked for more proof on the nature, clarity on the culture, and for Dion to
be added: the sacred city at the mountain's foot, where Macedonian kings
sacrificed to Zeus. A place with actual stones.

The Committee didn't wait. It voted yes, unanimously. Olympus was one of four
sites inscribed this year despite advice to hold off.

**[1:58–2:30] THE QUESTION**

So what did they actually protect? The experts wanted evidence you can dig
up. The Committee protected a story.

Maybe that's the honest answer: heritage was never really about stones. It's
about what we decide still matters.

But there's a catch. The gods' mountain was sacred because people stayed
below it. Now, in the Greek prime minister's words, it "belongs to all
humanity".

So what happens to a place that was holy because it was out of reach, once
all of humanity is invited up?

**Deliberately avoided:**

- **Visitor numbers.** Wikipedia says "over 10,000" a year; the Greek
  government says ~50,000. They're too far apart to quote.
- **Whether Dion ended up inside the final boundary.** Public statements are
  ambiguous, so the script only says the experts *asked* for it.
- **"Nobody worships these gods any more."** That isn't true. Modern Hellenic
  polytheists hold ceremonies on Olympus. The script says "once believed"
  instead.
